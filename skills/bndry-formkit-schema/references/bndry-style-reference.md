# BNDRY style reference

A working style guide for BNDRY help-centre articles, customer communications, support tickets, blog posts, and internal documentation. Built on the [Australian Government Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions) (CC BY 4.0) and tuned to the names, terminology, and content patterns BNDRY actually uses.

When in doubt, the Style Manual is authoritative. This document is a quick-lookup; links throughout go to source pages.

---

## At a glance

1. **Australian English.** `colour`, `organisation`, `licence` (noun), `licensing`, `program` (always — see the spelling section). Macquarie Dictionary as the tiebreaker.
2. **Active voice as default.** *Submit the report to AUSTRAC within 10 business days*, not *The report must be submitted to AUSTRAC...*
3. **Plain language.** Average 15 words per sentence, max 25. One idea per paragraph. Compliance officers at venues are not lawyers — write for them, not regulators.
4. **Minimal punctuation, minimal capitalisation.** Default to lowercase. Default to no comma. Default to no hyphen. Add them only when they pull their weight.
5. **No full stops in shortened forms.** `BNDRY`, `KYC`, `AML/CTF`, `Dr`, `Pty Ltd`, `NSW`. Exceptions: Latin (`i.e.`, `e.g.`) and `no.` for `number`.
6. **Numerals from 2; words for `zero` and `one`** — unless it's a measurement, time, percentage, or sentence opener.
7. **Single quotation marks** for direct speech and special-sense use. Double inside single for nested quotes.
8. **Apostrophes for possession and contractions only.** Never for plurals (`ABNs`, `MPs`, `the 1990s`, `RSLs` — no apostrophe).
9. **Italicise primary legislation titles.** *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (Cth).
10. **BNDRY voice is direct, clear, and respectful of the reader's time.** No filler, no hedging, no "we'd like to take this opportunity to..."

## Names BNDRY uses

### BNDRY product and entity

| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| `BNDRY` | The product and brand. Always all caps. |
| `BNDRY Pty Ltd` | The legal entity (ABN 49 678 808 449). Use in formal contracts, terms of service, and privacy policies. |
| `Identitii Limited` | The parent ASX-listed entity (`ASX: ID8`). Capital `I` and `L`; double `i` in `Identitii`. |
| `Cherryhub` | Data partner. One word, capital `C` only — the org writes it as `Cherryhub`, not `CherryHub`. |

### Australian regulators and government bodies

Treat each as a singular entity (`AUSTRAC is...`, not `AUSTRAC are...`). Spell out on first use in customer-facing content where the audience might not know the acronym; use the short form after that.

| First mention | Short form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre (AUSTRAC) | AUSTRAC | Acronym, pronounced as a word. |
| Attorney-General's Department | AGD or the department | Hyphen in `Attorney-General`. Possessive `Attorney-General's`. |
| Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) | OAIC | The body administering the *Privacy Act*. |
| Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) | ASIC | Initialism. |
| Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) | APRA | Acronym. |
| Liquor & Gaming NSW | Liquor & Gaming NSW or L&GNSW | Note the ampersand — that's how they write it. |
| Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC) | VGCCC | Replaced the former VCGLR. |
| Department of Home Affairs | Home Affairs or the department | Don't use `DHA`. |

When mentioning a regulator generically, use lowercase: `the regulator`, `the commission`, `the department`. Capitalise only when using the formal name.

### Legislation

Primary legislation: italicise the title, include the year, and add the jurisdiction in roman in parentheses on first reference.

- *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (Cth) — usually shortened to *the AML/CTF Act* in subsequent mentions.
- *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth) — usually *the Privacy Act* after first mention.
- *Identity Verification Services Act 2023* (Cth) — *the IVS Act*.
- *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Rules Instrument 2007* (No. 1) — *the AML/CTF Rules*.

Delegated legislation (rules, regulations, instruments) is **not** italicised in current style — only primary Acts are. Bills aren't italicised either (they become Acts on passage).

The Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) are a defined term — capital `A` and `P` when referring to them as a set. Individual principles: `APP 1`, `APP 5` (no italics, capital APP).

## Apostrophes in compliance terminology

[Source: Apostrophes](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/apostrophes)

The trickiest call BNDRY content makes daily: descriptive vs possessive in plural-noun phrases.

### The general rule

If the first noun *describes* the second (functions as an adjective), no apostrophe. If the first noun *possesses* the second, use an apostrophe.

### Patterns BNDRY hits often

| Descriptive (no apostrophe) | Possessive (apostrophe) |
|---|---|
| `customer details` (the details we hold about customers) | `the customer's signature` (one customer's specific signature) |
| `members register` (a register of members) | `the members' entitlements` (entitlements belonging to the members) |
| `patron records` (records about patrons) | `the patron's identity` (one patron's identity) |
| `workers compensation` (a type of insurance) | `the worker's claim` (one worker's claim) |
| `drivers licences` (a category of document) | `the driver's photo` (the photo on a specific driver's licence) |
| `directors duties` (general concept) | `the directors' resolution` (the resolution this board passed) |

Test by asking: *who or what is doing the possessing?* If the answer is "no one — these nouns are stacked", drop the apostrophe.

### Shortened forms

- Possessive of an acronym: apostrophe and `s`. `AUSTRAC's reporting requirements`, `BNDRY's privacy policy`, `the ANU's policy`.
- Plural of an acronym: just `s`. `ABNs`, `TTRs`, `RSLs`, `MPs`. Never `ABN's`.
- Don't introduce a shortened form in possessive form on first use. Write `the Australian Business Number (ABN)` first; only later use `the ABN's holder`.

### Place names

Australian place names with possessive sense drop the apostrophe: `Kings Cross`, `Bankers Trust Park`. Customer venues will sometimes have older naming — write each name as the venue currently uses it.

### Plurals of time periods

These are descriptive, not possessive — no apostrophe:

- `6 months pregnant`
- `in 4 days time`
- `5 years experience`

Singular time references take an apostrophe:

- `a day's notice`
- `a month's leave`

### Possessive pronouns

No apostrophe. Ever.

- `its` (belonging to it) — `the venue and its compliance officer`
- `it's` (it is or it has) — `it's been 30 days since the report was filed`
- `whose`, `yours`, `hers`, `theirs` — no apostrophes

## Numbers, dates, currency

### Numerals or words

[Source: Choosing numerals or words](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words)

- **Words** for `zero` and `one` in body text. *One report*, *zero exceptions*.
- **Numerals** for 2 and above. *2 forms of ID*, *14 days*, *30 business days*.
- **Always numerals** for: measurements, times, percentages with `%`, ages, decimals, and money.
- **Always words** when starting a sentence (or rewrite).

### Currency

[Source: Currency](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/currency)

- Dollar sign immediately before the numeral, no space: `$10,000`.
- For the AML/CTF threshold transaction value: `$10,000 or more` (the legal phrasing — `or more`, not `over` or `above`).
- Always two decimal places when including cents: `$10,000.00`. Leading zero for amounts under a dollar: `$0.50`.
- Cents alone: `75c` (no space, no full stop).
- For ambiguous contexts (offshore customers, foreign banking), prefix with `A$` for Australian dollars or use `AUD`. Don't mix the symbol and the IBAN code.

### Dates

[Source: Dates and time](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)

- Body text: **day month year**, spelt out: `13 May 2026`. Non-breaking space between day and month.
- Tables and constrained space: `13/5/2026` or `13/05/2026`. Day-first, never US-style month-first.
- Compliance records that must be machine-readable: ISO 8601 (`2026-05-13`).
- Decades: `the 1990s`. No apostrophe.
- Year ranges in body text: `from 2022 to 2025` or `between 2022 and 2025`. En dash (`2022–2025`) only in tables, headings, or technical content.
- Always specify `financial year` vs `calendar year` unless context makes it obvious — *FY2025* and *2025* mean different things.

### Time

- `9 am`, `2.30 pm` — lowercase, space after the numeral, full stop in `2.30` (not colon).
- 24-hour: `09:00`, `14:30`.
- Time zones for customer comms (national audience): use `AEDT` or `AEST` as appropriate. State it: *2 pm AEDT*. Don't assume the reader is in Sydney.

### Phone numbers

[Source: Telephone numbers](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/telephone-numbers)

Group in readable chunks. Link with `tel:` in web content.

- Mobile: `0412 345 678`
- Sydney landline: `(02) 1234 5678`
- 1300/1800: `1300 123 456`
- International (when writing for overseas customers): `+61 2 1234 5678`

### Percentages

- Numeral plus `%`, no space: `5%`, `99.9%`.
- `per cent` (two words) in body text where the symbol would look out of place: *Fifty-five per cent of venues...*
- Don't start a sentence with `5%` — rewrite or spell out.

## Shortened forms

[Source: Shortened words and phrases](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases)

BNDRY content uses a lot of these. Define on first use; short form thereafter.

### Acronyms and initialisms

- **Acronym** — read as a word: `AUSTRAC`, `ANZAC`, `MVSE`.
- **Initialism** — read letter by letter: `ASIC`, `APRA`, `MP`, `RSL`, `KYC`, `BNDRY`.
- No full stops between letters.
- `a` or `an` before the short form depends on the *sound*, not the letter: *an AUSTRAC report* (`an` because `AUSTRAC` starts with a vowel sound), *an SMR* (`an` because `S` starts with the sound `ess`), *a KYC check* (`a` because `K` starts with the sound `kay`).
- Plural: just add `s`. `TTRs`, `SMRs`, `IFTIs`.
- Possessive: apostrophe and `s`. `AUSTRAC's website`.

### Contractions

Two types, two different rules:

1. **Single-word contractions** — no apostrophe, no full stop: `Dr`, `Mr`, `Mrs`, `Mx`, `Pty`, `Ltd`, `St`, `Cth`, `no.` (note: `no.` keeps the full stop, alone among single-word contractions).
2. **Grammatical contractions** — apostrophe in place of missing letters: `don't`, `it's`, `we're`, `they've`.

BNDRY content style:

- **Customer help articles, blog posts, in-app copy** — grammatical contractions are fine and make the content feel less stiff. Use them.
- **Privacy policy, terms of service, regulator-facing correspondence, formal reports** — avoid contractions. Write `do not`, `it is`, `we are`.

### Latin shortened forms

[Source: Latin shortened forms](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)

Prefer English. Write `for example` (not `e.g.`) and `that is` (not `i.e.`), especially in customer-facing content.

When you do use them:

- `e.g.` and `i.e.` keep their full stops.
- No comma after `e.g.` or `i.e.`
- `etc.` is redundant after `for example`, `such as`, or `including` — don't use both.
- `[sic]` marks an error in a quoted source; the italicised `sic` sits inside roman square brackets.

## Punctuation

[Source: Punctuation](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation)

### Apostrophes

See the **Apostrophes in compliance terminology** section above. Two jobs: possession and contractions. Never for plurals.

### Brackets and parentheses

[Source: Brackets and parentheses](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/brackets-and-parentheses)

- Parentheses `()` for definitions on first use, citations, asides.
- Square brackets `[]` to clarify quoted material or insert `[sic]`.
- Don't nest parentheses — if you need to, square brackets go inside parentheses. Better: rewrite.
- Test: if you can delete the bracketed content and the sentence still works, the brackets are right. Otherwise the content needs to be in the sentence proper.

### Colons

[Source: Colons](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/colons)

Colons introduce: examples, lists, contrasts, block quotations. Lowercase after a colon unless what follows is a proper noun or a full quoted sentence.

Don't use a colon when the introducing sentence runs naturally into the list (`The team includes John, Sarah, and Mei.` — no colon).

### Commas

[Source: Commas](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/commas)

- After an introductory phrase or clause: *Under the AML/CTF Act, reporting entities must...*
- In pairs around non-essential information: *AUSTRAC, the national AML/CTF regulator, requires...* (both commas).
- Before a coordinating conjunction joining two main clauses with different subjects.
- Australian Government style does **not** use the Oxford (serial) comma by default — only when needed for clarity.
- Don't follow `e.g.` or `i.e.` with a comma.
- Before tagged questions: *You filed the report, didn't you?*

### Dashes

[Source: Dashes](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)

Two kinds in editorial use:

- **Spaced en dash** (` – `) for parenthetical asides — Australian Government style uses this where US style uses em dash. *The threshold — currently $10,000 — applies to all cash transactions.*
- **Unspaced en dash** (`–`) for spans of numbers, dates, pages, in tables and technical content: `pages 12–15`, `2022–2025`. Never combine with `from`.
- **En dash** between two coordinate nouns: `Murray–Darling Basin`, `Sydney–Melbourne flight`.
- Different from a hyphen. Don't mix them up.

### Ellipses

[Source: Ellipses](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/ellipses)

Three dots `…`, with a space on either side. Marks omission in a quotation only — never for trailing-off effect or dramatic pause. Don't follow with a comma, full stop, or semicolon.

### Exclamation marks

[Source: Exclamation marks](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/exclamation-marks)

Not BNDRY voice. Avoid in customer comms, help articles, blog posts, and formal documents. The only legitimate use is when the character appears literally (e.g. listing allowed password characters).

### Forward slashes

[Source: Forward slashes](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/forward-slashes)

Used in: dates (`13/5/2026`), web addresses, `AML/CTF`, fractions, and a small number of shortened forms. Avoid `and/or` constructions — pick one and rewrite. Slashes ask the reader to do work.

### Full stops

[Source: Full stops](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/full-stops)

- Ends sentences that aren't questions.
- Marks decimals (`0.5`).
- Single space after, not double.
- **No full stops** in acronyms, initialisms, contractions, abbreviations — except `i.e.`, `e.g.`, `n.d.`, `no.`

### Hyphens

[Source: Hyphens](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/hyphens)

- Compound adjectives before a noun: hyphenated. *AML/CTF-compliant venue*, *real-time verification*, *risk-based approach*.
- After the noun: usually no hyphen. *The venue is AML/CTF compliant.*
- Adverb ending in `-ly` plus adjective: **no** hyphen. *A clearly written policy*, not *clearly-written*.
- Prefixes: usually solid (`prepay`, `nonresident`, `preconfigure`). Use a hyphen to avoid double vowels or for clarity (`re-cover` vs `recover`).
- When in doubt, check Macquarie.

### Question marks

[Source: Question marks](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/question-marks)

End direct questions. Indirect questions don't take a question mark (*He asked who would file the report.*).

Avoid questions as section headings in formal content — they read informally. Exception: customer-facing help articles where conversational tone helps. *How do I add a new patron?* is fine as an article title.

### Quotation marks

[Source: Quotation marks](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/quotation-marks)

- **Single quotation marks** by default.
- Double inside single for nested quotes.
- For long quotations, use block formatting (no quotation marks). HTML `<blockquote>` for web.
- Punctuation inside the quotation marks if it belongs to the quote; outside if it belongs to the surrounding sentence.
- `[sic]` (italics inside roman square brackets) marks errors in the original.
- Don't use quotation marks for emphasis — use bold or rewrite.

### Semicolons

[Source: Semicolons](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/semicolons)

Link two closely related main clauses, or separate items in a complex list where commas would be ambiguous. Use sparingly — usually two sentences are clearer. Never at the end of bullet list items.

## Italics

[Source: Italics](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/italics)

Italics contrast with roman type and draw attention. Overuse defeats the purpose. Italic letterforms are also harder for some readers.

**Use italics for:**

- Titles of primary legislation: *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (`(Cth)` stays roman).
- Titles of substantial works (books, reports, films, journals).
- The genus and species of plants and animals (not relevant for most BNDRY content).
- Foreign words and phrases not absorbed into Australian English.

**Do NOT italicise:**

- Delegated legislation (the AML/CTF Rules), regulations, instruments, or bills.
- Section numbers (`section 41`, `s 41`).
- The names of awards, programs, or company products.
- Acronyms and initialisms.
- BNDRY product feature names — use plain text or bold for emphasis instead.
- Whole stretches of text for emphasis — rewrite or bold the key word.

## Voice and tone for help articles

This is the bit that's BNDRY-specific rather than Style Manual.

### Audience

The reader is usually a venue compliance officer, duty manager, or floor staff member. They've opened the help article because something isn't working or they need to do a task. They're at work, the phone is ringing, and a patron is at the bar.

Write for that person. Not the lawyer who drafted the *AML/CTF Act*. Not the auditor reviewing the venue's program next quarter.

### Default to imperative for instructions

- *Select the patron from the search results.* (Imperative — direct.)
- Not *You should select the patron from the search results.* (Wordy.)
- Not *The patron should be selected from the search results.* (Passive, hides who's doing it.)

Imperative is also shorter, which means less scrolling on a mobile screen behind the bar.

### Headings as questions

For help articles only — never in formal documents. Mirror what the reader would actually type into a search box.

- *How do I add a new patron?*
- *What is a Threshold Transaction Report?*
- *Why is the DVS verification failing?*

### Capitalisation in the product

- Feature and screen names: initial capitals if the product UI capitalises them, lowercase otherwise. Pick once and stay consistent.
- Role names (`Compliance Officer`, `Venue Manager`, `Floor Staff`): treat as the BNDRY platform writes them. Capital case when they're proper role labels in the system; lowercase when generic (*a compliance officer at the venue*).
- Button text and menu items: match the product exactly, capitalisation and all. Wrap in bold or backticks: *Click **Save**.*
- Generic UI terms (`tab`, `button`, `screen`, `field`): lowercase. *Open the **Patrons** tab.*

### Sensitivities

- Patron identity data is the subject of half the help-centre content. Treat with care in the writing. Don't use real identifying details in examples — write *Jane Citizen* or *J. Patron*, not the name of an actual customer.
- Don't make the reader feel stupid for being confused. The compliance regime is genuinely complex; the writing should help, not condescend.
- When directing readers to a regulator (AUSTRAC, OAIC, state licensing), link to the actual URL and the relevant page. Don't make them search.

### Avoid these patterns

- *We are pleased to announce* / *We are excited to* — promotional fluff. Cut.
- *Please note that* — *please note* is filler. Just say the thing.
- *In order to* — *to* does the same job.
- *Utilise* — say *use*.
- *Leverage* — say *use*.
- *At this point in time* — say *now*.
- *Going forward* — say *from now on*, or cut entirely.
- *Reach out* — say *contact* or *email*.
- *In a timely manner* — say *quickly*, or give a specific timeframe.
- *Robust* / *seamless* / *best-in-class* — marketing words that say nothing.

## Words and word types (condensed)

### Adjectives

[Source: Adjectives](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/adjectives)

Use sparingly. Every adjective should earn its place. Compound adjectives before a noun usually take a hyphen (`risk-based approach`, `real-time alert`); `-ly` adverb plus adjective does NOT (`a clearly drafted policy`).

### Adverbs

[Source: Adverbs](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/adverbs)

Modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Sparingly. Most modifiers are deletable without loss.

### Conjunctions

[Source: Conjunctions](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/conjunctions)

- **Coordinating** (`and`, `but`, `or`, `so`) join equals.
- **Subordinating** (`because`, `when`, `if`, `although`) join dependent clauses to a main clause.
- **Correlative** (`either/or`, `neither/nor`, `both/and`) must connect grammatically parallel elements.
- Don't use `with` to bolt a clause onto a sentence. *Reporting volumes have increased, with venues filing more SMRs* → *Reporting volumes have increased. Venues are filing more SMRs.*

### Determiners (`a`, `an`, `the`)

[Source: Determiners](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/determiners)

`a` vs `an` follows sound, not letter:

- *an AUSTRAC report* (vowel sound)
- *an SMR* (`S` sounds like `ess`)
- *a KYC check* (`K` sounds like `kay`)
- *a one-off issue* (sounds like *wun*)
- *an hour* (silent `h`)
- *a hotel* (spoken `h`)
- *a unique identifier* (sounds like *yoo*)

### Nouns

[Source: Nouns](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/nouns)

Proper nouns take capitals; common nouns don't. Collective nouns (`the team`, `the board`) take a singular verb.

### Pronouns

[Source: Pronouns](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/pronouns)

Singular `they` is correct and gender-neutral, and takes a plural verb form (`they are`). Don't default to `he` or `she` for an unspecified person — use `they` or rewrite.

Test for `me` vs `I`: take out the other element. *The team and I attended* → *I attended* (correct). *Contact the support team or me* → *contact me* (correct).

### Verbs

[Source: Verbs](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/verbs)

Active voice as the default. Imperative mood for instructions. Tense consistent within a paragraph.

### Prepositions

[Source: Prepositions](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/types-words/prepositions)

Some words demand specific prepositions: *different to* or *different from* (not *different than*); *compare with* (similarities and differences) vs *compare to* (likening). Check the dictionary.

## Sentences and clauses (condensed)

[Source: Parts of sentences](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/parts-sentences)

- Subject–verb–object as the default order.
- Average 15 words per sentence; max 25. If you're over 25, find the break point.
- Simple sentences scan fastest. Use compound and complex sentences for variety, not for showing off.
- Place modifiers next to what they modify. *Only the compliance officer can approve TTRs* is different from *The compliance officer can only approve TTRs*.

### Clauses

[Source: Clauses](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/parts-sentences/clauses)

`if` vs `whether` — they're not interchangeable in writing.

- `if` introduces a condition: *If the patron consents, we run the DVS check.*
- `whether` introduces alternatives: *Tell the patron whether the check passed or failed.*

`that` vs `which` — `that` for restrictive clauses (essential to meaning, no commas); `which` for non-restrictive (parenthetical, with commas).

- *The report that you filed yesterday went through.* (restrictive — which report? the one filed yesterday)
- *The report, which you filed yesterday, went through.* (non-restrictive — there's one report being discussed; the filing time is extra information)

### Phrases

[Source: Phrases](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/parts-sentences/phrases)

Avoid noun trains (three or more stacked nouns or adjectives). *Real-time patron risk assessment summary screen* — break into a clause: *the screen that summarises real-time risk assessments for patrons*.

## Spelling

[Source: Spelling](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling) · [Common misspellings](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling/common-misspellings-and-word-confusion)

Australian English throughout. Pick one dictionary (Macquarie is the manual's recommendation) and follow the headword (first form listed).

- `-ise`, not `-ize`: `organise`, `realise`, `analyse`.
- `-our`, not `-or`: `colour`, `behaviour`, `flavour`.
- `-re`, not `-er`: `centre`, `metre`.
- `practice` (noun) vs `practise` (verb). *The venue's privacy practice is to...* / *We practise minimum data retention.*
- `licence` (noun) vs `license` (verb). *Renew your driver licence.* / *AUSTRAC licenses certain reporting entities.*
- `program`, not `programme`. BNDRY house style uses `program` throughout, aligning with the spelling used in the *AML/CTF Act* and consistent across all BNDRY content (compliance, software, plans, initiatives).

### Words easily confused

- `affect` (verb, influence) vs `effect` (noun, result; verb, bring about). *The change affected reporting.* / *The effect was significant.* / *Effect the changes.*
- `principal` (main; head) vs `principle` (rule, value).
- `complement` (completes) vs `compliment` (praises).
- `discreet` (tactful) vs `discrete` (separate).
- `its` (belonging to it) vs `it's` (it is).
- `comprise` — the whole comprises the parts. Never `comprised of`.
- `ensure` (make certain) vs `insure` (insurance).
- `assure` (tell someone confidently) vs `ensure` vs `insure`.
- `enquiry` (a question) vs `inquiry` (an investigation, especially formal).

## Quick decision tree for tricky cases

- **Is it a proper noun?** → Capital. **Generic?** → Lowercase.
- **Is it an abbreviation, acronym, initialism, or contraction?** → No full stops (except Latin and `no.`).
- **Is it a number 2 or higher in body text?** → Numeral. **Is it `zero` or `one`?** → Word (with exceptions for measurement, time, percentage).
- **Is the first noun describing the second?** → No apostrophe. **Possessing it?** → Apostrophe.
- **Is the modifier before the noun?** → Hyphen if it's a compound adjective. **After the noun?** → Usually no hyphen.
- **Is it primary Commonwealth or state legislation?** → Italicise title with year, jurisdiction roman. **Delegated?** → Roman.
- **Is the sentence over 25 words?** → Split it.
- **Are you about to write `please note` or `we are pleased to`?** → Don't.
